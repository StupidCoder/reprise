# Library descriptions and historical sources

Added at the user's request, 26 October 2026. This is externally sourced
editorial context, **not evidence for the native reconstructions**. Descriptions
are newly written summaries of the original games, not promises about current
Joyport feature coverage. The shell displays implementation status separately.
No original advertising copy is reproduced.

Years mean the first catalogued release on the platform shown, not the first
release of the game on any machine. Sort ascending, with title as the tie-breaker.
Regional launches and reissues can differ. The notes below record disputed dates.

## Fort Apocalypse — C64, 1983

- [Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/game/fort-apocalypse): 1983 C64 release,
  Steve Hales creator, Joe Vierra conversion; US Gold tape's 1984 copyright.
- [MobyGames credits for Steve Hales](https://www.mobygames.com/person/7583/steve-hales/credits/):
  Atari 1982 and C64 1983. Use the platform-specific 1983 date rather than the
  1982 date some general catalogues carry over from the Atari original.
- [Game overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Apocalypse): helicopter,
  underground fortress, prisoners and fuel.

Search results also incidentally exposed a short original-source header in a
Lemon64 forum snippet. That header was not used, and the forum was not opened.
No external implementation was consulted or used to change game behavior.

## Bruce Lee — C64, 1984

- [MobyGames C64 credits](https://www.mobygames.com/game/191/bruce-lee/credits/c64/):
  year and Fortier/Day credits.
- [Electronic Games, November 1984](https://www.ataricompendium.com/archives/magazines/electronic_games/electronic_games_nov84.pdf),
  “The Men Behind the Computerized Ninja: Fortier and Day”: Kelly Day's account
  of experimenting with joystick control of Green Yamo and adding the second
  player. Indexed interview extract consulted (full PDF exceeds browser limit).
  This is a story about the game's design, not a claim of platform-specific AI parity.
- Description follows the already reconstructed game's premise and room actions.

## Wizball — C64, 1987

- [Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/game/wizball): platform year and credits.
- [Antstream's Jon Hare interview](https://www.antstream.com/post/game-developer-interview-jon-hare):
  Hare and Yates' songwriting partnership, wizard-in-a-ball concept and coloring
  landscapes. Interview indexed extract consulted; the live page is script-rendered.
- Description follows the game's color-restoration premise.

## Mindwalker — Amiga, 1986

- [Original Commodore manual](https://openretro.org/file/d22eefdbeb23b36a95d402cd7334ed551439bac7/Mind_Walker_Manual_ocr.pdf): 1986 publication.
- [YAAM interview with Bill Williams, February 1989](https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1989/02/yaam/yaam-interview-bill-williams/):
  256 KB constraint, Synapse and Commodore licensing. Attributed recollection,
  not an independently reconstructed memory budget.
- [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_Walker): surreal mental landscape,
  changing forms and rebuilding mental health. Original spelling is Mind Walker;
  Joyport retains its existing catalogue title Mindwalker.

## Marble Madness — Amiga, 1986

- [MobyGames Amiga credits](https://www.mobygames.com/game/466/marble-madness/credits/amiga/):
  year, Larry Reed, Ian Gooding and Jim Nitchals.
- [Original EA packaging scan](https://www.cvxmelody.net/Marble%20Madness%20Apple%20II%20original%20folder%2C%20command%20summary%20%26%20disk%20%281986%20Electronic%20Arts%29.pdf):
  multi-platform credits distinguish the Amiga programmer from other conversions.
  Use MobyGames' spelling of Nitchals; package transcription differs.
- [Computer Gaming World, November 1986](https://usermanual.wiki/Document/ComputerGamingWorld198611issue32.516254073.pdf):
  contemporary description of the Amiga arcade conversion and its sloping courses.

## Rock 'n Roll — Amiga, 1989

- [Lemon Amiga](https://www.lemonamiga.com/game/rock-n-roll): year, Frank Prasse,
  Martin Gaksch, Chris Hülsbeck.
- [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_%27n%27_Roll_%28video_game%29):
  title's double meaning, mouse/joystick difference and hazards. No unverified
  production anecdote is added; this entry uses credits and edition context.

## Tower of Babel — Amiga, 1990

- [MobyGames releases](https://www.mobygames.com/game/17525/tower-of-babel/releases/):
  Atari ST 1989, Amiga 1990. This resolves the common generic 1989 attribution.
- [CU Amiga, April 1990](https://retrocdn.net/images/8/8b/CUAmiga_UK_002.pdf):
  contemporary review of the tower designer, palette, objects and four floors.
- [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&layout=layout_wap.htm&p=9102):
  overview of robot roles and programmed/direct control. Its broad 1989 date
  disagrees with platform-specific release listings; it is not used for the year.
- [Amiga Format, June 1990](https://malus.exotica.org.uk/~buzz/amr/af011_1990_06.pdf):
  contemporary review and playable coverdisk demo corroborate the Amiga launch period.

## Wings of Fury — Amiga, 1990

- [MobyGames releases](https://www.mobygames.com/game/2215/wings-of-fury/releases/):
  1990 Amiga conversion by Unlimited Software.
- [Platform credits](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/appleii/980172-wings-of-fury/credit):
  Steve Waldo, Colin Fox, John Boechler, Kris Hatlelid (also credited as Krisjan).
- [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_of_Fury): 1987 Apple II origin
  and carrier-based flight premise. No later fan ports were used.

## Cannon Fodder — Amiga, 1993

- [Jon Hare interview, Pixel Ninjas, 2022](https://pixel-ninjas.de/interview-jon-hare/):
  1993 release discussion and question 13's promotional video recollection.
  The anecdote is attributed to Hare; no claims of first-ever mechanics are adopted.
- Description summarizes the squad game and its named casualties, rather than
  copying its original packaging or lyrics.

All entries' brief on-screen source credits correspond to these references.

## Spy Mouse — iOS, 2011

- [Engadget, 8 March 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011-03-08-gdc-2011-firemints-agent-squeek.html): Firemint's Agent Squeek at GDC 2011.

## Sonic games — Game Gear and Game Boy Advance

Connected to Reprise on 2026-10-29 (games/sonic). Years are the first release on
the platform shown; regional dates differ and the sources disagree on some.

- Sonic the Hedgehog, Game Gear, 1991: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_(8-bit_video_game))
  and [MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/game/36523/sonic-the-hedgehog/): Japan 28 December 1991;
  Western dates given as October, November or December 1991.
- Sonic the Hedgehog 2, Game Gear, 1992: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_2_(8-bit_video_game)):
  Japan 21 November 1992; [MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/game/42573/sonic-the-hedgehog-2/releases/) lists Europe in October 1992.
- Sonic Triple Trouble, Game Gear, 1994: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog:_Triple_Trouble):
  November 1994 (Japan 11 November, as Sonic & Tails 2).
- Sonic Advance, Game Boy Advance, 2001: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Advance):
  Japan 20 December 2001; North America February 2002 (the imported USA cartridge).

- History notes: Ancient as Koshiro's studio, its first and only Sonic game
  ([Ancient (company)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_(company))); Aspect's
  separate 8-bit Sonic 2 and the first four-megabit Game Gear cartridge (Sonic 2
  article above); Fang/Nack's debut and the removed gun
  ([List of Sonic the Hedgehog characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sonic_the_Hedgehog_characters));
  Sonic Advance as Dimps' first Sonic game, sharing early Nintendo-platform status
  with Sonic Adventure 2: Battle ([Talk:Sonic Advance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Sonic_Advance)
  disputes "the first").

The card descriptions are the collection's original summaries.
