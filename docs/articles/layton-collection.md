# Layton collection: development stories

Research checked 30 October 2026. Six original seven-paragraph articles, following
[the shared editorial guide](../WRITING_DEVELOPMENT_ARTICLES.md). No direct quotes,
external illustrations or major mystery solutions. The European catalog names
are used; American names are supplied when needed to identify historical sources.

All six game.md files and their plan.md companions under
[games/layton/docs](../../games/layton/docs) were reviewed. Original-game
implementation claims below come only from those cartridge-based findings.
External historical sources were not used to reconstruct game logic. Current
port omissions, catalogue totals including extra puzzles and research hypotheses
are not presented as retail features or defects.

  visual research, Sherlock Holmes connection and replacing culturally dependent
  puzzles are Hino's testimony. Fewer than 200 derived words across the article.

  Full chapter read. First self-published Japanese work and a clear next objective
  for inexperienced/interrupted players are the account given by Hino and Iwata.
  P3's separation of intended and accidental difficulty is editorial interpretation.
- P5–7: [Curious Village game.md](../../games/layton/docs/curious_village_ds/game.md),
  Cartridge and program, Data formats and languages, GDS script machine, Trunk
  pages and minigames, Puzzles. Five-language European image, event/puzzle
  organisation, hotel, painting, dog, index and puzzle shack are local findings.
  These mechanisms are not claimed to prove Hino's unrecorded intentions.
