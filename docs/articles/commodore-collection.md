# The remaining Commodore development stories

Research checked 30 October 2026. These eight articles complete the current
13-game Commodore collection. Each has seven paragraphs. They use original prose,
with no direct quotations, scans or outside implementation material. Historical
interviews are testimony; mechanical observations come only from this project's
original-image research. Article paragraphs are numbered below in reading order.
No publication dates or commercial histories are inferred from Reprise's progress.

## Wizball — Painting a world into existence

Body: [Wizball](../../resources/articles/wizball_c64.txt).

- P2–4: [Jon Hare, Talkin'Games, 19 February 2019, chapter I, page 2](https://talkingames.com/jon-hare-chapter-i/2/).
  Relevant interview text read in full: musical collaboration, Defender/Dropzone,
  theatre lighting, paint, laboratory, Nifta and the later appearance of the name.
  Keep the combined account below 200 words derived from this source.
- P4, division of contributions: [Jon Hare interviewed by Peter Ward, Retro Gamesmaster](https://www.retrogamesmaster.co.uk/jon-hare-exclusive-interview/).
  Full interview read. Hare assigns bouncing ball/catellite to Yates and paint to
  himself. This is retrospective attribution, not a documented design chronology.
- P1, P5–7: [C64 game.md](../../games/wizball_c64/game.md), Levels, Catellite,
  Findings made while rebuilding. Reusable character blocks, palette restoration,
  following/steering and paint collection are local findings. P7 is editorial
  interpretation of those mechanics. The article does not say the game performs
  physically accurate RGB mixing or claim hardware limits caused the concept.

## Rock 'n' Roll — A maze with momentum

Body: [Rock 'n' Roll](../../resources/articles/rock_n_roll_amiga.txt).

- P2 credits: [Lemon Amiga's credit transcription](https://www.lemonamiga.com/game/rock-n-roll)
  and indexed [MobyGames credits](https://www.mobygames.com/game/2047/rock-n-roll/).
  Direct MobyGames credit-page access failed. The article conservatively calls
  Gaksch involved in production: databases differ between design and direction,
  and an ST credit must not be silently applied to Amiga. Do not infer an exact
  task split, original-platform ordering or tune authorship beyond the credit.
- P2–3, P6: [contemporary reviews transcribed by Amiga Reviews](https://www.amigareviews.leveluphost.com/rocknrol.htm):
  Green, Amiga Computing, January 1990, pp.28–29; Andy Smith, Amiga Format 6,
  January 1990, pp.64–65; Mark Mainwood, CU Amiga, December 1989, p.79.
  Relevant review text read. Under 200 article words derive from this page.
  Reviews' claims about lacking a save/restart feature are deliberately not
  repeated as fact: local research identifies level-code support.
- P1, P4–7: [Amiga game.md](../../games/rock_n_roll_amiga/game.md), Mouse to velocity,
  Level files, Cell rules, Shops, Overview map, Front end and Music and sound
  effects. Name-entry movement reuse and the sequenced music are local findings.
  The visual band introduction is absent from the supplied cracked disk; its
  absence is not described as an original development cut. No production
  interview establishing the concept's origin was located. The article therefore
  concentrates on the documented design and reception rather than inventing one.

## Wings of Fury — Relearning the flight of a Hellcat

Body: [Wings of Fury](../../resources/articles/wings_of_fury_amiga.txt).

- P2: [Broderbund, Wings of Fury User's Guide, Apple II edition](https://whatisthe2gs.apple2.org.za/files/WingsofFury/Manual/Wings_of_Fury-Users_Guide.pdf),
  printed About the Author page (PDF p.5). PDF text read. Biography is explicitly
  attributed to the publisher; no claim this was Waldo's first game.
- P3–5: [Game Not Over, episode 72, Wings of Fury, transcript](https://gamenotover.de/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/072-Wings-of-Fury.pdf),
  PDF pp.4 and 13. Relevant passages read: the author directly interviewed Colin
  Fox and John Boechler. Fox's first commercial Amiga project, lack of original
  files, playing the Apple II version, conversations with Waldo and roughly ten
  months are testimony reported by that interviewer. This is historical testimony
  about the original conversion, not consultation of a port's code or a reverse-
  engineering guide. Fewer than 200 words derive from this source.
  Conflicting recollections of the recorded voice remain unresolved in the article.
- P1, P6–7: [Amiga game.md](../../games/wings_of_fury_amiga/game.md), Flight model,
  1/8-scale view, Carrier animations, Islands and mission rules, Maps k–o / ending.
  The repeating m/n/o sequence is established for the supplied Amiga image; it is
  not attributed to the Apple II game, nor called an intentional design decision.

## Beach Volley — A sporting cartoon from Ocean France

Body: [Beach Volley](../../resources/articles/beach_volley_amiga.txt).

- P2–3: [Marc Djan interview, Codetapper](https://codetapper.com/amiga/interviews/marc-djan/).
  Relevant historical interview read: starting Ocean France, Operation Wolf /
  Dragon Ninja, seeking projects, Beach Volley among games based on arcade ideas,
  architecture/Beaux-Arts and visual priorities. Under 200 derived words.
  Only the historical interview is used; no linked technical analyses, asset maps
  or disassemblies were consulted. No specific arcade inspiration or formal
  licence is asserted, because Djan does not name one for Beach Volley here.
- P4: established catalog credits, corroborated by
  [Lemon Amiga](https://www.lemonamiga.com/game/beach-volley).
  Credits were read in the search-index transcription; direct page access returned
  403. Do not substitute Atari ST programmer Alexis Leseigneur for Amiga programmer
  Michel Janicki. Individual scene authorship is not established by broad credits.
- P1, P5–7: [Amiga game.md](../../games/beach_volley_amiga/game.md), Disk, Intro,
  Courts and Music. Separate sections, seven comic pages, six voices, travel
  files, eight wide court images and score written into scenery are local evidence.
  Court locations inferred from pictures are not presented as official names.
  P7 is editorial synthesis, not testimony about particular production meetings.
